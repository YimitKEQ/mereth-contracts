Two ideas carry the weight

The first

Escrow or a handshake, and everyone can see which

Fund it and the gold leaves your purse the second you post, held until the job is done, so whoever takes it knows they are getting paid. Do not fund it and nothing moves, so anyone taking that job is betting on you.

That one visible flag turns reputation from a number nobody looks at into a question people actually ask each other: will anyone work for you on your word alone?

The second

Stiff someone and it follows you around

Job gets done, client cannot pay, and nothing errors out. The server writes a sealed debt note with both names on it and the job it came from, and anyone can read it. A broken promise stops being an error message and starts being evidence.

When someone cannot pay

server/contracts/contract.ts

The engine is pure. It never touches the game, so it tests headless in milliseconds, and it never decides who was in the right.

Gold never appears from nowhere

server/contracts/escrow.ts

Two rules for anything touching money. Never mint gold: every septim paid out came off somebody first. And take it before you promise it, so when something breaks you end up with no contract instead of one nobody can pay.

You cannot act as someone else

server/contracts/wire.ts

The client sends an action and a contract id. It never sends who it is. That comes off the connection, and that is the whole auth story right there.

Why everyone was called Prisoner

server/contracts/identity.ts

My favourite bug in this thing. Every player character sits on the same base record, the vanilla player NPC_ at 0x7, and that record is named Prisoner. So getActorName cheerfully told me Prisoner had posted a job for Prisoner and Prisoner had taken it. Took a live server to spot.

The real name lives in the character's appearance, and it is the same string SkyMP floats above their head: the client calls refr.setDisplayName(model.appearance.name, true) on every appearance update. The identity that actually sticks is profileId. Form ids get reshuffled every time someone connects.

Four things that will eat your afternoon

undocumented

Four things gate the server to client channel, and every one of them fails silently. No error, no warning, the client just never hears about your properties. None of them are documented, and the first only exists as a string inside scam_native.node.

GateWhat you see when it is wrong
enableGamemodeDataUpdatesBroadcast Server logs skipping gamemode data update send and never ships the channel.
server-info-ignore createClientSafe :0. The client resolves no peer and never connects at all.
serverKey + server-public-keys Pushed JavaScript is rejected with no signature found and deleted.
Debug.messageBox A modal box pauses the game loop, and updateOwner only runs on update. Every push freezes while it is on screen.

Who decides the job is done

not honour

A funded contract is sitting on real gold between "I am done" and "here is your pay". Whoever gets to end that standoff on their own can rob the other one, so it comes down to what was promised.

What was promisedHow it ends
Goods, funded Machine checked. The server counts the items out of the worker and into the client before it will record a delivery, then pays immediately. Nobody is taken at their word.
Service, funded The worker reports it done and the escrow stays held. The client has two days to accept or refuse. Say nothing and it releases to the worker.
Service, unfunded The client settles it themselves. If they cannot pay, a sealed debt note is written naming both parties.

Middle row is the interesting one, and I had it wrong at first. A funded service used to pay out the instant the worker hit the button, so you could take a funded job, claim it done, and walk off with the escrow having done nothing. Expiry let you run the same scam from the other side: ignore a finished job till the deadline and your gold comes back.

Expiry will not touch delivered work now, and the review window shuts the other hole. A refusal still gets logged against both of you and the server judges neither, because that part should stay between players.

Not guessing. Your client is public in BStarRP_SkyMP_Data, so I checked every mechanism this needs against the actual 7.5 MB bundle your launcher ships, not against upstream skymp.

What this needsIn your client

updateGamemodeDataMessagePresent. The channel that carries them is intact.
ServerJsVerificationServicePresent, with the skymp:sig:y: prefix. Signing works unchanged.
eventSourceContexts, sendEventPresent. Player actions reach the server by the same route.

SweetTaffyEvalServiceRemoved. Only my unattended test harness used it. The feature does not.

Here is the one that would have bitten you. Your client drives its own React UI with browser.loadUrl and browser.setVisible all over the place, so anything of mine grabbing the browser would have wiped your interface mid session. It does not. The push calls a global if one is there and fires a CustomEvent, which is exactly how your client already feeds your own UI. Both do nothing if nobody is listening.

So one listener on mereth:contracts and your UI has the board. Or skip that, drop in the standalone panel above, and write nothing at all.

Getting it running

four steps, no coordination with me

You do not need me for any of this. Download the folder, wire four lines into your gamemode, flip two settings, and the server tells you on boot whether it is working.

Do not take my word for it

before you trust a line of it

The engine tests itself against your live server every time it boots, on your ESPM data and your database. No harness, no fixtures, nothing to install. Start it and read the log.

That posts real contracts, locks up real gold, defaults one on purpose, writes the debt note, then checks the maths. If it says anything other than CONTRACTS OK, do not ship it, and the failing line tells you what broke.

  • Then go try to break it. Post a funded service, mark it delivered as the worker, and watch the escrow sit there refusing to move until the client answers.
  • Try to rob yourself. Take your own contract, settle work you never did, refuse a delivery you had nothing to do with. Server knocks back every one and tells you why in plain words.
  • Pull the plug. Contracts live in server properties, so kill it mid contract and the board comes back exactly how you left it.
  • Read it. 1,293 lines of TypeScript across six files, nothing imported outside node:crypto and the SkyMP types. The interesting decisions carry a comment saying why, not what, which is most of the remaining bulk and is deliberate: you should not have to take my word for any of it.

What it does not do

  • Goods are checked, services are not. The server counts items into the client's hands. A service settles on the worker's word on purpose, because that is the bit players should be arguing about, not the server.
  • No arbitration. No dispute queue, no staff tooling. A rejection gets logged against both parties and judged by neither.
  • Chromium depends on the install. Both Skyrim Platform browser backends fought me on my dev box, so the console board is what I could screenshot there. You already pin NirnLab UI Platform, so the panel above is the path on yours.
  • Tested on a local server. Real skymp5-server, real ESPM data, one connected client. Not yet against your load or your modlist.
